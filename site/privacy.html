Privacy policy

Last updated October 20, 2026 · Pax 1.0

The short version

  • Everything you do in Pax stays on your phone. Pax has no accounts and sends none of your data to a server.
  • Pax has no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Deleting the app deletes your data.

Who we are

Pax is a daily Catholic companion app made by an independent developer, Ben Brunson. Questions about this policy are welcome through the support page.

What Pax keeps on your phone

Pax keeps two databases inside its private storage on your phone.

The library holds the app's reading content: the Douay-Rheims Bible, prayers, devotions, the saints, reading plans and the Church calendar. It is the same for everyone and contains nothing about you.

Your own data holds:

  • your settings (appearance, theme color, daily rhythm, Rosary options, where you last read);
  • which days you read, prayed or studied, your streak and the grace days you used;
  • your reminder choices and times;
  • the novenas and reading plans you started, and how far along you are;
  • your journal, notes on verses, highlights, bookmarks and favorite prayers;
  • prayers you add to your prayer book.

This data never leaves your phone. Your journal and your own prayers are never part of a widget or a notification.

Widgets

So Pax's home-screen and Lock Screen widgets are ready whenever you add one, Pax copies what they show into storage that only Pax and its widgets can read: the next seven days of the Church calendar (each day's name, color, saint and reading citations), your current streak, today's Read, Pray and Study marks and the days you kept this week, whether you prayed today's Rosary, the novena or reading plan you are on and which day, the day's "word for today", and pictures of Pax. This copy stays on your phone. Widgets show it on your home and Lock Screen, where anyone holding your phone can see it; remove a widget to stop showing it.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled on your phone; no server sends them. Their text can name the day's feast or the novena you are praying, and can appear on your Lock Screen. You can turn them off in Pax (Me → Reminders) or in your phone's Settings.

When Pax uses the internet

  • App updates. When Pax opens, it asks Expo's update service whether a newer version of the app's code is available. That request carries the app's version, the platform and a random installation ID created by the update software; it is not linked to you or to anything you do in Pax. Expo uses it to deliver updates and count how many installs have each one.
  • The Church Fathers library is an optional download, made only when you tap Download. The request carries no personal data.
  • Links you tap to outside pages (the full readings on usccb.org, the Catechism on vatican.va, the source of a Father's excerpt) open in an in-app browser, where those sites' own privacy policies apply.

Like any internet request, these reveal your IP address to the server that answers them. Pax sends nothing you store in the app.

Sharing

The share button on a saint's page passes the saint's name and a short summary to your phone's share sheet. Pax sends nothing itself; you choose the app and the person.

Children

Pax is suitable for all ages. Because it collects no personal data, it collects none from children.

Deleting your data

  • Everything: delete the Pax app. That removes your data, the widgets' copy and every scheduled reminder.
  • Parts of it: journal entries, your own prayers, novenas and reading plans can each be deleted in the app.

Changes to this policy

If Pax ever adds optional features that store data beyond your phone, such as syncing between devices, this policy will be updated before that feature arrives, and such features will always be your choice.
